Pepper 0.3 records only where you say so

Earlier versions saved your prompts and replies on every AI site without asking. That has stopped. Nothing new is recorded until you turn learning on for a site below.

Kept records only count toward learning on sites where you turn learning on.

Your rough prompt, written out properly.

Type what you want in a few words. Pepper rewrites it into six clear parts using only what you've told it about yourself, then puts it in the chat box. You read it and press send.

  1. 1

    Let Pepper learn what works for you

    With learning on for a site, Pepper saves the prompts you send there, the reply (up to 4,000 characters) and the stars you give it. It uses them to pick the approach that gets your best ratings, to aim for the length you like, to suggest prompts that worked before, and, if you add a model key, to offer facts about you to remember. Everything stays in this browser.

    Off by default. Prompt writing works either way. You can change this per site at any time.

  2. 2

    Add your model key optional

    With your own Anthropic or OpenAI key, your model writes the prompt and it reads much better. Without one, Pepper uses a built-in template that works offline.

    When a key is set, what you type, your profile answers, active memories and the text of your project's files are sent to that provider to write the prompt. The key is stored only in this browser and sent only to that provider.

  3. 3

    Three things about you
